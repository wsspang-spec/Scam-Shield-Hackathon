# Score Speech Acts, Not Keywords, to Catch Scam Calls

The single cleanest discriminator between a scam call and a legitimate one is the direction of information flow combined with the exit path the caller allows: scammers ask the callee to supply secrets (OTP read-back, PIN, full SSN, card number) or to move money over an irreversible rail (gift cards, crypto kiosk, wire to a new "safe" account, cash or gold to a courier), and they resist any attempt to hang up and call back, while legitimate fraud departments, collectors, the IRS and medical offices confirm facts they already hold, ask yes/no questions about transactions, and invite a callback to a published number. Authority impersonation is present in roughly 95% of documented vishing cases, so an authority claim on an unsolicited inbound call is the right trigger to start watching but a poor trigger to alert on, because banks, the IRS and clinics make authority claims too ([Jones et al. 2020](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10281805)). The published failure mode of real-time LLM detectors is exactly this confusion: recall of 0.98 to 1.00 but precision of only 0.70 to 0.77 on legitimate calls that mention "payment" or "ID," which an explicit criteria list plus an UNCERTAIN tier raises to about 0.85 ([Shen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03964v1)). No shipping product (Google Pixel Scam Detection, Hiya AI Phone, Truecaller) publishes precision, recall or time-to-alert, and every academic corpus is Chinese, Korean, Turkish or synthetic, so a one-day build that reports utterance-to-alert latency on English hard negatives, explains which rubric items fired, and flags a mismatch between claimed identity and call metadata is claiming genuinely open ground ([Google support](https://support.google.com/phoneapp/answer/15654065?hl=en); [LREC 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.lrec-1.585.pdf)). The archetypes worth scripting for a demo, ranked by dollars and phone share, are bank fraud-department impersonation, government impersonation (SSA, jury duty, Phantom Hacker handoffs), tech support, family emergency, and the robocall-to-agent Amazon refund, with imposter scams alone taking over $3.5 billion in 2025 ([FTC June 2026](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/06/ftc-data-show-people-reported-losing-3-point-5-billion-imposter-scams-2025)). The report closes with a weighted rubric and a FRAUD/UNCERTAIN/SAFE prompt you can paste in today.

## Information direction, exit path and payment rail carry the signal; tone and urgency do not

The persuasion literature tells you what scammers rely on but not what separates them from honest callers. Coding 86 real vishing cases, Jones and colleagues found authority in 95.3%, social proof in 90.7%, distraction in 89.5%, liking in 75.6% and commitment or reciprocity in only 25.6%, with the most consistent concrete element being "claiming to be from a reputable institution" ([Jones et al. 2020](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10281805)). Two findings from that study reshape how a rubric should treat urgency. Scammers rarely said they themselves were in a hurry; instead they said the information or the problem was time-sensitive. And a controlled experiment on 3,000 university phones found that **fear versus reward framing made no significant difference** to compliance, while the entity impersonated was the strongest factor (d = 0.331), so a prize hook and a problem hook deserve identical weight ([Tu et al. 2019](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec19-tu.pdf)). Urgency directed at an object ("the account will be locked," "the warrant will be issued," "the refund expires in three days") is the scam pattern; a busy agent saying "I only have a minute" is not.

The strongest structural evidence comes from two corpora. A Hidden Markov Model fitted to 341 scam-baiter transcripts (about 90 hours, 15,234 scammer utterances) recovered the same four-stage arc across SSN, refund, tech-support and reward scams: a character-specific greeting, a problem explanation, step-by-step instructions (remote access, forms, websites), then financial exploitation, with SSN scams adding "redirection to supervisor" and "providing case ID" as named sub-stages ([Wood et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.01965)). A RoBERTa classifier on that corpus reached **F1 of 92 to 98% on scam type from five or six utterances**, which is the empirical basis for scoring a rolling window of roughly that size rather than single lines. The second corpus, an AI honeypot that took 10,211 inbound scam calls over 54 days, found callers requested identity anchors such as home address and date of birth far more often than payment credentials, relied on "persistence and manufactured authority rather than overt threats," and that escalation was predictable at **0.72 ROC-AUC from the first line and 0.87 by the eighth** ([Traister et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24127)). Early turns are informative; you do not need to wait for the word "gift card."

Conversational control is the next tier of signal and it is nearly exclusive to scams. Forensic analysis of IRS scam recordings documented interruption ("Can I just..." answered with a flat "No"), deferral of the victim's informational questions to "later," answering questions with questions, directive speech acts (withdraw cash, go to the store, buy vouchers), and polar tag questions ("OK?", "All right?") used 7 to 33 times per sample to extract agreement ([Tabron 2016](https://www.blackhat.com/docs/us-16/materials/us-16-Tabron-Language-Properties-Of-Phone-Scammers-Cyberdefense-At-The-Level-Of-The-Human.pdf)). The scam-baiter corpus corroborates it with lines like "Do not interrupt me in between" and "There is no need to worry because I'm here to help you" ([Wood et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.01965)). The FBI lists "keeping victims on the phone for the duration of the scam" and "urging victims not to tell family, friends, financial institutions, or law enforcement" as defining markers ([IC3 PSA 2026](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2026/PSA260917)), and the SSA Inspector General states that an intra-call transfer to SSA or OIG "is a scam, without exception, every time" and that the agency "will never ask you to keep the call a secret" ([SSA OIG 2026](https://oig.ssa.gov/assets/uploads/2026-july-fake-call-transfers.pdf)). Stay-on-the-line, do-not-tell-anyone, and handoff-to-a-second-persona are therefore high-weight items with no documented legitimate counterpart.

Payment rail is near-dispositive and the agencies are unanimous. The FTC's fourth sign of a scam is insistence on cryptocurrency, a wire service, a payment app or gift cards "including giving them the card numbers," and "honest businesses" never require these ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/system/files/consumer_ftc_gov/pdf/How-to-avoid-a-scam_English_August2023.pdf)). The IRS will not demand a prepaid debit card, gift card or wire, and any check payable to anyone but "U.S. Treasury" is a tell ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/node/59151)). The FBI adds prepaid cards, couriers, bank wires, crypto and "cash deposited into cryptocurrency kiosks," and will never ask you to "use large sums of their own money to help catch a criminal" ([IC3 PSA 2026](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2026/PSA260917); [FBI Detroit](https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/detroit/news/press-releases/fbi-detroit-warns-public-to-beware-of-government-impersonation-scams)). The dollars confirm the priority: among older adults reporting $10K+ impersonation losses in 2024, crypto (mostly Bitcoin ATMs) was 33% of payments, bank transfer 20% and cash 16%, and crypto kiosk complaints to the FBI hit $389M in 2025 ([FTC Aug 2025](https://ftc.gov/news-events/data-visualizations/data-spotlight/2025/08/false-alarm-real-scam-how-scammers-are-stealing-older-adults-life-savings); [IC3 2025](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)). "Safe account," "segregated holding account," "federal protection" and "move your money to protect it" belong in the same bucket; no real FTC employee will say it ([Hacked.com citing FTC](https://hacked.com/move-your-money-to-protect-it-scam/)).

Two warnings about what not to score. Pure keyword classifiers look spectacular and then collapse: TF-IDF models hit F1 0.996 on a Korean corpus but lost up to 31 points of accuracy when an LLM paraphrased scammer lines to sound casual ([Li et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.16291)), and a Random Forest that scored F1 1.00 on public data fell to 0.26 when both classes shared the same top-50 terms ([Shen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11643v2)). Score the speech act ("directive to purchase," "request to read back a code," "instruction to conceal"), not the token. Second, do not treat calm as evidence of legitimacy. Tech-support scammers were "patient, kind" and 60% stayed polite when challenged ([Miramirkhani et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.06891.pdf)); bank impersonators use "a calm, reassuring tone to build trust while creating urgency" ([First Mid Bank](https://www.firstmid.com/articles/your-bank-will-never-ask-you-this)). Aggression is sufficient but never necessary, so its absence must not lower the score.

## Legitimate fraud teams use the same words, so the rubric must pivot on what they never do

The known false-positive categories from real-time LLM detection are legitimate identity verification, legitimate financial information sharing, and legitimate urgent decision-making; the canonical example is an airline rebooking call flagged at utterance 15 because the agent mentioned a "payment" for a change fee ([Shen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03964v1)). Keyword-seeded normal dialogues produced the most false positives, which is precisely what a bank's real fraud department sounds like. The fix is to make the discriminating criteria the things institutions say they never do, and to score the legitimacy behaviors as negative evidence rather than merely ignoring them.

| Caller type | What the legitimate version does | What makes it a scam |
|---|---|---|
| Bank fraud department | Asks if you recognize a specific transaction, verifies with data it already holds, may lock the card, "encourages you to hang up and call the number on the back of your card" ([First Mid Bank](https://www.firstmid.com/articles/your-bank-will-never-ask-you-this)) | Asks you to read back a one-time code, PIN, CVV, password or full card number; tells you to send money "to yourself" or to a "safe account"; resists callback ([TRB](https://trb.bank/all/bank-fraud-department-impersonation-scam/); [Zelle](https://www.zelle.com/how-to-spot-a-pay-yourself-scam)) |
| Debt collector | Gives name, company, street address, phone, license number where required; provides debt details at first contact or within five days; you can dispute ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-the-fair-debt-collection-practices-act-en-1699/)) | Threatens arrest or criminal charges, refuses a mailing address, withholds debt details, demands payment on a four-hour deadline, asks for full SSN or financial data ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2017/08/phantom-debt-collectors-impersonate-law-firms?page=5)) |
| IRS | Mails first ("generally you receive several letters first"), presents ID, offers a dedicated IRS number to verify, offers several payment options and a chance to question or appeal ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/node/59151)) | Demands a specific method (gift card, prepaid, wire), threatens police or immigration arrest or license revocation, recites a badge number, yells ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/tax-season-protect-yourself-irs-imposter-scams/)) |
| SSA / law enforcement | Never calls to demand payment or sensitive info, never receives a transferred call, never asks for secrecy ([SSA OIG 2026](https://oig.ssa.gov/assets/uploads/2026-july-fake-call-transfers.pdf); [IC3 PSA 2026](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2026/PSA260917)) | "Your SSN is linked to a crime," warrant for missed jury duty, bond payable by crypto ATM, "a sergeant is monitoring you" ([AARP 2025](https://www.aarp.org/podcasts/the-perfect-scam/info-2025/jury-duty-crypto-atm-scam.html)) |
| Medicare / medical | Medicare "will not call you to verify or update your Medicare number or to enroll you in a plan" ([WA OIC 2025](https://www.insurance.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-10/medicare-fraud-oep-fact-sheet.pdf)) | "New card being issued," "preapproved for a cheaper plan," "refund for overpayment," asks for Medicare number, SSN or bank details |

The distinguishing rule that falls out of every row is the same: a legitimate caller is willing to be verified and gives you a path to do it independently, while a scammer must be verified on their own terms and on this call. The FTC's framing is "honest organizations won't call, email, or text to ask for your personal information" and "honest businesses give you time to decide" ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/system/files/consumer_ftc_gov/pdf/How-to-avoid-a-scam_English_August2023.pdf)). For the LLM this translates to a set of explicit negative-evidence items: caller offers or accepts a callback to a published number, verifies with yes/no questions about transactions rather than asking for identifiers, offers written notice or an appeal, volunteers full identifying details, declines to take payment details on the call, and the callee initiated the contact. Each should subtract from the score. One under-specified area to flag honestly: regulator guidance covers banks and government thoroughly but says little about legitimate utility, school or medical-billing calls, which share urgency and payment vocabulary; your hard-negative set should include at least one of each so judges see them pass.

Timing is the second half of the false-positive problem. Shen and colleagues found that adding an UNCERTAIN output raised precision from 0.70 to 0.85 but delayed the alert on a police-impersonation call from utterance 6 to utterance 10 ([Shen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03964v1)). The operational lesson is to separate "watching" from "warning": let authority claims and urgency raise an internal score silently, and reserve the visible alert for a request that the callee supply a secret, move money, conceal the call, or stay on the line. Hysteresis over two or three consecutive windows keeps a single stray "payment" from firing the banner.

## Only two products analyze live content and none publish metrics, which leaves the gap open

The consumer market splits into three layers that rarely combine. Carriers work on network metadata: T-Mobile Scam Shield "primarily relies on the STIR/SHAKEN caller ID protocol" plus ML on spoofing and robo-dialers, and users report false positives that caused them to miss legitimate calls ([SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/2200747/tmobile-report-spam-feature-effectiveness/)). AT&T flags "multiple short-duration calls to numbers on the National Do Not Call list" and keeps humans reviewing daily so school districts are not cut off ([AT&T](https://www.att.com/es-us/sdabout/pages/cyberaware/ni/robocall)). Apple's iOS 26 Call Screening is a pre-answer gatekeeper that asks unknown callers for a name and reason and shows the transcript; it performs no scam classification and known contacts bypass it ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/phones/iphone/call-screening-in-ios-26-has-finally-ended-my-spam-call-nightmare-heres-how-to-set-it-up)). Truecaller's AI Call Scanner is a manually triggered human-versus-synthetic voice check with no published accuracy ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2024/05/29/truecaller-ai-call-scanner/)).

Only Google and Hiya analyze conversation content during the call. Pixel Scam Detection runs Gemini Nano on-device, watches for patterns such as "someone posing as a bank representative urgently asking for fund transfers, gift card payments, or PINs," is off by default, plays a beep at call start and every few minutes so participants know it is on, offers "End call" and "Not a scam" buttons, stores nothing, and Google states only that it "isn't 100% accurate" ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/02/26/pixel-scam-detection-messages-phone/); [Google support](https://support.google.com/phoneapp/answer/15654065?hl=en)). Hiya AI Phone "analyzes call audio for scam language" and AI-voice artifacts, again with no metrics ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250128511230/en)). Enterprise deepfake vendors are no more transparent: Pindrop markets "99% accuracy" and "2300+ detection signals" without methodology, and Reality Defender's RealCall notably returns an "uncertain" result when audio cannot support a verdict ([Pindrop](https://pindrop.com/products/liveness-detection); [Reality Defender](https://www.realitydefender.com/product/realcall)). ElevenLabs' own classifier covers only ElevenLabs-generated audio at 99% precision and 80% recall and has no API ([ElevenLabs docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-creative/audio-tools/audio-detector)), so voice-liveness is the wrong thing to build from scratch in a day.

The papers give you the recipe but not the English benchmark. The real-time Chinese study scored after each turn with FRAUD/SAFE or FRAUD/UNCERTAIN/SAFE prompts ([Shen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03964v1)). The Korean fine-tuning work split transcripts into blocks, had the model score each block 0 to 10, and compared a length-weighted average to a tuned threshold; adding 11 expert-written criteria to the prompt kept GPT-4o at 99.11% on an adversarial set where a KoBERT baseline collapsed to 56.25%, while chain-of-thought did not help the 8B model ([arXiv 2506.06180](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06180v1)). The LREC 2026 Korean benchmark showed whole-call accuracy of 97.5% for Qwen2.5-72B but 38.1% for Gemma-3-1B, and the authors say detection "requires contextual reasoning and dialogue structure understanding" and that their evaluation "does not test real-time detection or continuous monitoring" ([LREC 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.lrec-1.585.pdf)). ConvoSentinel reached F1 0.74 after just five messages by combining a message-level sensitive-request detector with retrieval of similar past snippets ([arXiv 2406.12263](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12263v2)). Reassuringly for a hackathon pipeline, a Turkish study found uncorrected ElevenLabs Scribe transcripts cost only about 0.008 F1 versus human-corrected ones, and that raw audio fed to multimodal LLMs triggered safety-filter refusals on aggressive police-impersonation calls, so classify on text ([arXiv 2606.24523](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24523)).

The claimable gap is therefore specific. No shipping product or paper demonstrates, end to end, a live English transcript scorer that explains which rubric items fired, exposes a confidence tier with hysteresis, checks the claimed identity in the transcript against call metadata (unknown number, number not matching the institution's published line, STIR/SHAKEN attestation below A when you run on a VoIP line where the SIP Identity header is readable), and reports measured utterance-to-first-alert latency and precision on a hard-negative set. The infrastructure exists: Scribe v2 Realtime is a WebSocket endpoint with roughly 150 ms latency, accepts 8 kHz mu-law so Twilio media streams pipe straight in, emits committed transcripts you can append to a rolling window, and costs $0.28 per hour ([ElevenLabs docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-api/guides/how-to/speech-to-text/realtime/server-side-streaming.md); [ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/realtime-speech-to-text)). Use dual-channel audio rather than relying on diarization, since the scammer's channel carries the signal and realtime diarization support is unconfirmed. Borrow Google's consent template (opt-in, periodic tone, per-call off switch, nothing stored, "Not a scam" button) because thirteen states including Massachusetts, California and Florida require all-party consent and no guidance yet says whether unstored real-time transcription counts as recording ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/employment-law-watch/102ls2n/the-legality-of-ai-powered-recording-and-transcription/)). Demo on scripted, consented calls.

## Five archetypes carry the dollars and give you demo scripts

Phone is only 11% of 2025 loss reports by first contact, but it is the top channel for adults 80+ and the costliest channel for older adults, with a median phone-initiated loss of $2,210 versus $650 for social media ([FTC April 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/data-visualizations/data-spotlight/2026/04/reported-losses-scams-social-media-eight-times-higher-2020); [FTC Dec 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P144400-OlderAdultsReportDec2025.pdf)). Within phone-initiated fraud against older adults, business impersonation is 40% of losses, government impersonation 23% and prize or sweepstakes 15%. The FBI's "call center fraud" lines (tech support plus government impersonation) exceeded 80,000 complaints and $2.9 billion in 2025, with tech support losses climbing from $924M in 2023 to $2.134B ([IC3 2025](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)). Medicare calls dominate by volume with a low average loss of $539 ([Hiya Q4 2024](https://www.hiya.com/newsroom/press-releases/ai-deepfake-fraud-calls-dominate-q4-scams-costing-consumers-millions)). Voice cloning is real but small in dollars (about $5M in grandparent scams in 2025) and changes nothing about the script, so your detector covers it for free if it scores structure ([IC3 2025](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)).

The following script skeletons are assembled from quoted lines in the sources and are safe to adapt into synthetic transcripts. Write each as a two-speaker dialogue of 15 to 30 turns, put the pressure items after turn 5, and write a paired legitimate twin for each.

**Bank fraud department (OTP read-back).** Caller ID shows the bank. "This is the fraud department. We detected suspicious activity. Did you authorize a $1,200 charge at Best Buy in Dallas?" After the no: "We need to secure the account right away. We're going to send you a security code. Please read it back to me so we can verify your identity." Then: "Just read the code back to me and we'll lock the account," and if the callee hesitates, "We want to stop the charge before it goes through." Zelle variant: "To reverse this, send the money to yourself" and "I need the enrollment code to authorize it" ([Reliabank](https://www.reliabank.com/when-the-fraud-department-is-the-fraud/); [TRB](https://trb.bank/all/bank-fraud-department-impersonation-scam/); [Zelle](https://www.zelle.com/how-to-spot-a-pay-yourself-scam)). The legitimate twin asks the same opening question, says "I'm going to lock the card now, you'll get a new one in five to seven days," and closes with "if you'd like to confirm this call, hang up and dial the number on the back of your card."

**SSA / government impersonation.** "My name is Officer Carol Snyder with the Social Security Administration. This is the case identification number, please write it down." "Your Social has been used to open accounts that are now used to launder approximately $240,000." "Can you verify me the last four digits of your Social Security number?" "Do not interrupt me in between." "Let me connect you to my senior agent." "If you don't cooperate I will send a local sheriff on your doorstep" ([Wood et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.01965)). Jury-duty variant: "You missed jury duty yesterday and there is a warrant for failure to appear. The bond is $9,000. You cannot hang up or talk to anyone. A sergeant is monitoring your drive. Withdraw the cash and deposit it at the kiosk using this QR code. If you don't do this right now, you're going to get arrested" ([AARP 2025](https://www.aarp.org/podcasts/the-perfect-scam/info-2025/jury-duty-crypto-atm-scam.html)).

**Tech support and Phantom Hacker handoff.** "Your computer has been sending error reports. Download this program so I can run a scan." Fake diagnosis lines: "These services were stopped by hackers," "You can see the virus detected, your system is at risk," "These are attackers connected to your machine" ([Miramirkhani et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.06891.pdf)). Then the Phantom Hacker pivot: "Open your bank accounts so we can check for unauthorized charges. Your bank's fraud department will call you shortly." Second persona: "A foreign hacker has accessed your accounts. We need to move the money to a safe account at the Federal Reserve. Do not tell anyone the real reason you are moving these funds" ([IC3 PSA 2023](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2023/PSA230929)).

**Amazon refund robocall to agent.** Recorded opening: "Thank you for shopping with Amazon. Your iPhone purchase has been confirmed. A total amount of $1,299 will be charged to your account. If you did not authorize it, press 1." Live agent: "Before I can cancel the order, I need to verify the account holder." "Read the code to me so I can cancel the iPhone order." After remote access: "We refunded $2,999 instead of $1,299. You need to return the difference in gift cards. Do not call your bank yet" ([MalwareTips](https://malwaretips.com/blogs/amazon-iphone-purchase-phone-call-scam-exposed-full-investigation/)).

**Family emergency.** "Grandma, it's me. I'm sorry I sound different, I broke my nose in the accident." Handoff: "This is Officer Daniels. Your grandson has been arrested and needs bail money. Please don't tell his parents, the judge has issued a gag order. A bond agent will come to your door to collect the cash tonight" ([National Cybersecurity Alliance](https://staysafeonline.org/articles/when-scammers-call-grandma)). Add a phantom-debt and a utility-shutoff variant if time allows: "I have four hours to settle up or an arrest warrant goes out" and "the disconnection truck is already in your area, the technician cannot be recalled after the deadline" ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2017/08/phantom-debt-collectors-impersonate-law-firms?page=5); [MalwareTips](https://malwaretips.com/blogs/?p=402713)).

Keep two facts from the honeypot work in mind while writing: scammers spent about 15% more turns per decade of apparent target age, and they requested sensitive information in 26.3% of calls regardless of age ([Traister et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24127)). Scammers also dominate talk time, about 1,367 words to the victim's 530 in tech-support calls ([Miramirkhani et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.06891.pdf)), so make your synthetic scam dialogues lopsided and your legitimate twins balanced.

## A weighted rubric and tier logic you can paste into the prompt

The design below follows what the evidence supports: a 0 to 100 score built from criteria the LLM marks present or absent per rolling window, legitimacy behaviors scored as negative evidence, a three-tier output, and hysteresis before alerting. Weights are a judgment call informed by the strength and exclusivity of each signal in the sources, not fitted coefficients; no paper publishes per-feature importances, so tune them on your own hard negatives.

| Tier | Criterion (score the speech act, not the word) | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Dispositive | Asks callee to read back, text or type a one-time code, PIN, password, CVV or full card or SSN | +35 |
| Dispositive | Directs payment via gift cards, crypto kiosk or wallet, wire to a new or "safe" account, cash or gold to a courier, prepaid card, or "send money to yourself" | +35 |
| Dispositive | Instructs secrecy ("don't tell anyone," "don't call your bank yet," "gag order") | +30 |
| Dispositive | Transfers or hands off within the call to a second authority persona (supervisor, fraud department, SSA, Federal Reserve, officer) | +25 |
| Strong | Instructs callee to stay on the line, not hang up, or keeps them on while they travel to a bank or store | +25 |
| Strong | Requests remote-access software install or screen sharing | +25 |
| Strong | Threat of arrest, warrant, lawsuit, deportation, license loss, account suspension, or "sheriff dispatched" | +20 |
| Strong | Refuses or deflects callback or independent verification; becomes hostile or escalates when questioned | +20 |
| Moderate | Asks callee to supply identity anchors (DOB, address, account number) rather than confirming ones the caller holds | +15 |
| Moderate | Unsolicited authority claim with props (case ID, badge number, officer or agent title) | +12 |
| Moderate | Problem or prize hook on first contact (unauthorized charge, hacked device, SSN linked to crime, won a prize, owed a refund) | +10 |
| Moderate | Object-directed deadline ("account locks in 30 minutes," "warrant issues today") | +10 |
| Weak | Conversational control: interruption, "do not interrupt," repeated tag questions, deflecting the callee's questions | +8 |
| Weak | Overpayment or "we refunded too much" ploy | +10 |
| Negative | Caller invites callback to a number on the card, statement or official site | -30 |
| Negative | Verifies with yes/no questions about specific transactions; states they will not ask for codes | -20 |
| Negative | Offers written notice, dispute or appeal, time to decide, or several payment options | -15 |
| Negative | Callee initiated the call, or caller is a saved contact | -20 |
| Negative | Caller volunteers full name, company, address and license or reference without being asked | -10 |
| Metadata | Claimed institution does not match number reputation or published line; attestation B or C when available | +15 |

Tier logic: raw score below 20 is SAFE, 20 to 54 is UNCERTAIN, 55 and above is FRAUD. Any single dispositive item plus one strong item should push to FRAUD regardless of the arithmetic. Show the visible alert only when FRAUD holds for two consecutive windows or a dispositive item fires alone, and show a quiet "watching" indicator for UNCERTAIN. Re-score every committed transcript segment over the last roughly six turns plus a one-paragraph running summary of earlier turns, since scam-type F1 reaches 92 to 98% at five or six utterances ([Wood et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.01965)).

```text
You are a real-time phone scam detector. You receive the running transcript of a
call in progress, labeled CALLER (the other party) and USER (the person we protect),
plus metadata: inbound/outbound, saved contact yes/no, claimed institution if any.

Score the CALLER's behavior with the rubric below. Score speech acts and intent,
never isolated words. "Payment," "verify," "urgent" and "fraud department" are
NOT evidence by themselves; legitimate banks, collectors, the IRS and clinics use
them. Calm, polite tone is NOT evidence of legitimacy.

DISPOSITIVE (+35 code/PIN/password/full card or SSN read-back request;
+35 payment via gift card, crypto kiosk, wire to new or "safe" account,
courier cash or gold, prepaid card, "pay yourself"; +30 secrecy instruction;
+25 in-call handoff to a second authority persona)
STRONG (+25 stay on the line or do not hang up; +25 remote access or screen
share; +20 threat of arrest, warrant, lawsuit, deportation, suspension;
+20 refuses or deflects callback, escalates when questioned)
MODERATE (+15 asks USER to supply DOB, address or account number instead of
confirming data already held; +12 unsolicited authority claim with case ID,
badge or title; +10 problem or prize hook; +10 object-directed deadline;
+10 overpayment or refund-too-much ploy)
WEAK (+8 interruption, "do not interrupt," repeated "OK?" tag questions,
deflecting USER's questions)
NEGATIVE (-30 invites callback to a published or on-card number; -20 verifies
by yes/no on specific transactions or says it will never ask for codes;
-15 offers written notice, appeal, time, or multiple payment options;
-20 USER initiated the call or CALLER is a saved contact; -10 volunteers full
identifying details unprompted)
METADATA (+15 claimed institution inconsistent with the number or attestation)

Return JSON only:
{"score": 0-100, "tier": "SAFE"|"UNCERTAIN"|"FRAUD",
 "fired": [{"criterion": "...", "quote": "exact caller words", "turn": n}],
 "negatives": [{"criterion": "...", "quote": "...", "turn": n}],
 "archetype": "bank_impersonation|government_impersonation|tech_support|
               family_emergency|refund_overpayment|debt_collection|
               utility|medicare|prize|other|none",
 "user_action": "one sentence the USER should do right now, or empty"}

Tiers: <20 SAFE, 20-54 UNCERTAIN, >=55 FRAUD. Any dispositive item plus any
strong item is FRAUD regardless of total. If no CALLER request directed at the
USER to supply, send, install, conceal or stay has yet occurred, cap the tier at
UNCERTAIN even if the score is high.
```

The last line encodes the single most important lesson from the real-time precision results: authority and urgency open the window, but a request directed at the user to act is what should close it. The "quote" fields are what make your alert explainable on screen, the "user_action" field is where you surface the FBI's behavioral defenses (hang up, call back on an independently found number, never share a 2FA code, use a family secret word) ([IC3 PSA 2025](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2025/PSA250515)), and the "Not a scam" button on the UI is the one in-product precision signal any shipping system has, so log it.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the problem from "detect scam language" to "detect an asymmetric transaction": one party is being asked to hand over secrets or irreversible value, under instructions that prevent them from checking with anyone else. Every legitimate counterpart, from a Chase fraud analyst to an IRS revenue officer to a hospital billing clerk, is institutionally required to leave the callee an exit, and that exit is the discriminator a one-day build can score reliably. It also means voice cloning, the most headline-grabbing threat, is already covered by a structural detector, while acoustic liveness is the one feature you should explicitly decline to claim.

The competitive opening is transparency rather than accuracy. Google and Hiya almost certainly have higher raw recall than anything you ship tomorrow, but neither tells the user which behavior tripped the alarm, neither reports a number, and neither cross-checks the caller's story against the call's metadata. A demo that shows a live score climbing turn by turn, cites the exact words that moved it, holds back on the legitimate twin of each scam, and prints "first alert at utterance 7, 140 ms after commit" is doing something no published system does, and the hard-negative calls are what will make judges believe it.
